The Wayfarers Brotherhood

Faction document · Norrath campaign · Est. year ~11,800

Overview

The Wayfarers Brotherhood is a Norrath-wide organization of professional adventurers, investigators, and specialists who take contracts that official military forces cannot, will not, or dare not handle themselves. They operate in the space between civilization's reach and the wilderness beyond it — politically neutral, racially inclusive, and answerable only to their own internal code and their paying clients.

They are not mercenaries in the crude sense. A mercenary sells violence. The Brotherhood sells capability — and capability sometimes means violence, sometimes means scholarship, sometimes means knowing when to walk away.

Origins

The Brotherhood was founded sometime around year 11,800 by a loose coalition of veteran adventurers who had survived the Crusade of Tears and found themselves without a war to fight, without a home to return to, and without the temperament for ordinary life. The founders — whose names are recorded in the Brotherhood's founding charter but rarely spoken — recognized that Norrath had a permanent need for capable people willing to go into dangerous places for reasonable pay.

The original charter established three principles that remain in force today: neutrality in city politics, acceptance of any race willing to work by the code, and refusal of contracts that require harm to innocents. The third principle has been tested more than the other two.

What makes them useful to cities like Freeport

Deniability: A city that hires the Brotherhood can claim no official involvement in whatever happens next. Lucan uses this constantly.
Unusual capability: The Brotherhood specifically recruits people who are, as their intake assessment puts it, "difficult to categorize." Spellcasters, former criminals, the magically sensitive, the racially mixed. People the regular army wouldn't take.
No questions asked (within limits): The Brotherhood does not require clients to explain why they want something investigated. They do require clients to be honest about the danger involved. Contracts that understate risk are voided and the client is blacklisted.
Speed: A Brotherhood team can be assembled and deployed faster than any military unit. They have no chain of command to navigate beyond the local chapter master.

What they will not do

Assassinate named political targets
Take contracts from both sides of an active conflict simultaneously
Harm civilians or destroy civilian infrastructure
Surrender a Brotherhood member to a client's justice without Brotherhood review
Work for the Ring of Scale directly — the only faction explicitly blacklisted in the founding charter, for reasons the Brotherhood does not publicly discuss

Reputation across Norrath

The Brotherhood is regarded with a mix of respect and unease by most city governments. They are useful and they know it, which gives them a negotiating leverage that officials find irritating. Among common people they are generally well-regarded — the Brotherhood has a tradition of taking a small number of pro bono contracts each year for communities that cannot pay, which generates goodwill that money cannot buy.

Among adventurers, membership is considered a mark of competence. Being turned down by the Brotherhood is not shameful — their standards are high. Being expelled is.

The party's rank

The player characters begin as Pathfinders — full members with the right to negotiate contracts but not yet senior enough to be assigned their own teams. This is deliberate. Pathfinder rank means the characters have a history with the Brotherhood (each player can define one prior contract they completed to earn the rank) but are not so senior that being hired by Kell feels beneath them.

The code

"We go where others will not. We do what others cannot. We come back when others do not. We remember what others prefer to forget."

The code is recited at formal Brotherhood gatherings and is engraved above the door of every chapter house. Members treat it with varying degrees of reverence — the old hands tend to take it seriously; newer members sometimes find it theatrical. The old hands are usually right.

Pay structure

Standard split: 70% to the team, 20% to the chapter house (maintenance, supplies, legal costs), 10% to the Brotherhood's central fund (pro bono contracts, member death benefits)
Hazard pay: Contracts involving undead, planar entities, or magical anomalies carry a 25% hazard premium, negotiated before acceptance
Death benefit: If a member dies on contract, the Brotherhood pays 200 gp to their named beneficiary and handles remains if recoverable
Dispute resolution: If a client refuses to pay, the Brotherhood's legal fund pursues collection. Chapter Masters have been known to pursue this personally.

The Freeport chapter

The Freeport chapter of the Wayfarers Brotherhood operates out of a converted warehouse in the West Commons, three blocks from the main market and two blocks from a Militia checkpoint — a location that was chosen deliberately. Being visible to the Militia keeps relations cordial. Being in the Commons rather than inside the city walls keeps the Brotherhood's independence credible.

The chapter house is called The Waystation. It has a common room, a contract board, six private briefing rooms, a small infirmary, a secured vault for evidence and sensitive materials, and sleeping quarters for up to twenty members. It smells permanently of old maps and someone's pipe tobacco.

The chapter's relationship with Lucan

Complicated. The Militia uses the Brotherhood regularly enough that both parties have developed a working arrangement — the Brotherhood takes Militia contracts, the Militia does not interfere with Brotherhood operations within Freeport's walls, and both parties pretend the other is more trustworthy than they actually are.

The current chapter master, Orvyn Dast, knows that this arrangement has a ceiling. Lucan tolerates the Brotherhood because they are useful. The moment they become inconvenient, the tolerance ends. Dast has a contingency plan for that day and does not discuss it with anyone except the two people he trusts completely.

Current contracts active in Freeport

The Kell contract: The Militia has engaged the Brotherhood to investigate Befallen and locate a missing researcher. Standard rate plus 25% hazard premium for undead presence. This is the party's current contract.
Missing persons — East Freeport docks: Eleven dock workers have vanished over three months. The Militia claims it is investigating. It is not. A Brotherhood Pathfinder team has been working this for six weeks with no resolution.
Merchant escort — Commonlands road: Three merchant houses have pooled resources to hire Brotherhood escorts after two caravans were lost to unknown attackers. Ongoing.
The Halfling problem: A representative of the halfling fishing community south of Freeport — the same community near the proposed Iksar settlement site — has approached the Brotherhood asking for help. They cannot pay much. Dast accepted anyway.

The Waystation — notable locations

The contract board

A large corkboard covered in sealed envelopes, pinned requests, and a running list of active assignments. Members can post their own leads. Dast reviews and approves everything before it goes up.

The vault

DC 18 lock, magical ward (DC 16 Arcana to identify, DC 14 dispel). Contains evidence from closed contracts, sensitive client documents, and — locked in a secondary box inside — a copy of the founding charter.

The common room

Open to members and guests. Cheap food, decent ale, a fireplace, and three tables permanently occupied by members playing cards. Good place to hear rumors.

Dast's office

Always unlocked during business hours. Two chairs, a desk buried in paperwork, a map of Antonica on the wall with pins marking active contracts. Dast does not like people touching the map.

The contract system

How contracts work

Intake: Client approaches the chapter house and submits a written brief. Dast or a designated Trailblazer reviews it for scope, risk level, and ethical compliance. Most contracts are approved within a day.
Assignment: Dast matches the contract to available members by capability and rank. Pathfinders and above can negotiate terms. Wayfarers take what they are assigned.
Briefing: The team receives a sealed dossier with everything the client has disclosed. What the client has not disclosed is noted explicitly as unknown — the Brotherhood does not pretend to know more than it does.
Completion: Team delivers a written report on return. Report goes into the vault. Client receives a summary. The full report is Brotherhood property.
Disputes: If team members disagree with a contract's ethics mid-execution, they can invoke a Code Review — a formal process that pauses the contract and requires a Trailblazer ruling within 48 hours. Used rarely. Taken seriously.

Contract tiers

Tier I — Routine

Escort, investigation, retrieval. Standard pay. Appropriate for Wayfarers and new Pathfinders. The Commonlands escort is Tier I.

Tier II — Elevated

Significant danger, political sensitivity, or magical involvement. Hazard premium applies. Pathfinder minimum. The Befallen contract is Tier II.

Tier III — Critical

Existential stakes, planar involvement, or direct confrontation with major factions. Trailblazer minimum. Requires Dast's personal sign-off.

Tier IV — Classified

Known only to the chapter master and The Navigators. Three Tier IV contracts have been issued in the Brotherhood's history. Their nature is not recorded in any accessible document.

Player hooks and character integration

Why are they Pathfinders

Each player character has completed at least one prior contract for the Brotherhood that earned them Pathfinder rank. Ask each player to briefly describe theirs — what was the contract, where was it, what happened, and did anything about it stay with them. This prior contract can establish:

A contact: Someone they met on the previous job who might reappear
A loose end: Something from the prior contract that was never fully resolved
A reputation: The Brotherhood knows something specific about this character's capabilities or tendencies
A debt: Someone helped them on the prior contract. Or someone didn't.

What the Brotherhood gives the party

Institutional backing: When the party identifies themselves as Brotherhood members, most people in the civilized world know what that means. It opens some doors.
The contract board: Between major story beats, the party can take secondary contracts from the board. These are opportunities for worldbuilding, character development, and reward without derailing the main narrative.
Information network: The Brotherhood has members across Norrath. Dast can, with time, get answers to questions the party cannot easily research themselves.
A home base: The Waystation is a safe place in Freeport. Given what Freeport is becoming under Lucan, this matters more than it might initially seem.
Moral framework: The code gives the party a shared ethical baseline without being prescriptive. When they face a difficult choice, "what does the Brotherhood do here" is a useful question that players can answer for themselves.

What could go wrong for the party within the Brotherhood

Expulsion: Violating the code — especially harming innocents or taking a blacklisted contract — triggers a formal review. Three Trailblazers hear the case. Expulsion is permanent and publicized.
Lucan closes the chapter: If the party's actions make the Brotherhood politically untenable in Freeport, Lucan could simply shut the Waystation down. Dast has a contingency. It involves leaving Freeport.
The Navigators disagrees: If the party's handling of the Befallen information conflicts with what The Navigators decides is strategically correct, there will be tension. The Navigators has authority Dast does not.
A member turns: Someone in the Freeport chapter is passing information to the Militia. Dast doesn't know who. The party might find out before he does.

Advancement within the Brotherhood

Promotion from Pathfinder to Trailblazer requires completing three Tier II contracts with commendations, a positive vote from two sitting Trailblazers, and Dast's approval. Given the trajectory of this campaign, the party will have earned it long before Dast formally offers it. When he does, the timing will be meaningful.
